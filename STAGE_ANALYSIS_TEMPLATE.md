# Stage analysis notes — completed first pass

The stage order below is from the recruiter's supplied guide. The examples in the application are illustrative phrasings based on recurring themes, not verbatim transcript quotations. The private transcript ZIP is not included in the public repository.

| # | Stage | What the screen should communicate | Common response patterns | Quick result to record |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opening | Reach the right person, introduce yourself, verify the contact, and recognize when they are unavailable. | Name/company needs repeating; caller is asked to try later; contact has left. | Right person; reception; callback requested; wrong/moved contact. |
| 2 | Purpose | Explain why you called in one or two clear sentences. Answer questions about the offer or source of the contact directly. | Asks what prompted the call; asks what the company does; questions where contact details came from. | Asked reason; asked company; asked source; purpose understood. |
| 3 | Permission | Establish if the prospect is willing to speak now. Separate permission, a callback, and a request for written information. | Gives brief permission; asks for a callback after a meeting; requests context first. | Continue; callback requested; information first; not willing now. |
| 4 | Their business | Learn the company's actual work, customers, products, and the prospect's remit before pitching. | Describes business; corrects a mistaken assumption; says another division or head office owns the topic. | Explained business; corrected assumption; asked about caller; context not a fit. |
| 5 | Their need, in their words | Record the goal, constraint, or absence of need using the prospect's actual wording. | Wants to prioritize a product line; says current customers are enough; describes pressure on margins. | Goal/challenge stated; no need; asked what caller can do; unclear. |
| 6 | Objection | Understand the reason for resistance without arguing. Respect clear refusals and do-not-call requests. | Existing group-wide team; wrong owner/contact; wants written information before deciding. | Existing provider; wrong contact; written information requested; clear no. |
| 7 | Next step | Agree one concrete action with a time, owner, or channel. Do not treat document receipt as proof of buying interest. | Gives a callback day/time; accepts report by preferred channel; refers to another colleague. | Time agreed; report/information agreed; referral; no concrete commitment. |
| 8 | Close | Confirm the agreed action or record accurately that there was a refusal, disconnect, or no outcome. | Confirms a time/channel; declines politely; line/audio fails. | Commitment confirmed; declined; line dropped; possible outcome mismatch. |

## Cross-transcript observations

- The index covers 100 calls and 1,945 dialogue turns. Median recorded duration is 93.5 seconds; 68% of calls are at most two minutes long. This supports short prompts and low typing overhead.
- Logged outcomes count: agreed to receive report (14), follow-up (14), not interested (14), not logged (13), next step agreed (11), do not call (10), wrong person (8), interested (7), moved company (5), call again (4).
- The transcript readme explicitly warns that the logged outcome can differ from the conversation. The interface therefore keeps stage evidence separate from the final caller-reviewed outcome.
- Calls can stop because the person is wrong/unavailable, the offer does not fit, the prospect refuses, or the line is poor; other calls end after a report handoff or callback time. The transcript files have no stage-boundary annotations, so this first pass does not invent exact stage-end counts.

## Ambiguities and safeguards

- “Go ahead” is permission to continue, not evidence of buying interest.
- “Send me something” may lead to a useful document handoff, but is weaker than a confirmed meeting or time-bound follow-up.
- “Not the right person” may indicate a referral opportunity, but the contact may decline to share another person's details.
- A warm goodbye does not invalidate a refusal; a poor connection is not a rejection.
- The AI may suggest a stage or summary but must not invent the prospect's need, commitment, or sentiment. The caller reviews all generated fields before saving.
